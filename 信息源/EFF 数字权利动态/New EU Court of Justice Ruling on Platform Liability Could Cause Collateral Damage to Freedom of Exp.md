# New EU Court of Justice Ruling on Platform Liability Could Cause Collateral Damage to Freedom of Expression

> 数据源：EFF 数字权利动态
> RSS：[打开源文件](https://www.eff.org/rss/updates.xml)

> 作者：Christoph Schmon　|　发布时间：Wed, 22 Jul 2026 08:34:12 +0000　|　分类：Free Speech　|　原文：[打开链接](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/07/new-eu-court-justice-ruling-platform-liability-could-cause-collateral-damage)

------

<div class="field field--name-body field--type-text-with-summary field--label-hidden"><div class="field__items"><div class="field__item even"><p><span data-contrast="auto">Intermediary liability laws </span><a href="https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/05/platform-liability-trends-around-globe-taxonomy-and-tools-intermediary-liability"><span data-contrast="none">around the world</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> </span><a href="https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/03/rewriting-intermediary-liability-law-what-eff-asks-and-you-should-too"><span data-contrast="none">recognize</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> that social media platforms, search engines, and other online service providers have become an integral part of our lives: they shape how we access information, communicate with others and participate in public debate, and foster innovation online. These laws generally shield platforms, to varying degrees, from legal liability for user content: the responsibility for unlawful speech should rest primarily with the speaker, not with those who merely host it. </span><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">These</span> liability protections are <a href="https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/05/platform-liability-trends-around-globe-safe-harbors-increased-responsibility">not a gift</a> for platforms.<b><span data-contrast="auto"> </span></b><span data-contrast="auto">They exist so that platforms</span><span data-contrast="auto"> are not encouraged to </span><a href="https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/08/general-monitoring-not-answer-problem-online-harms"><span data-contrast="none">proactively monitor and filter</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> what we say online, or to remove even lawful speech simply to avoid legal risk.</span><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<p><b><span data-contrast="auto">This is why a recent judgment by the EU Court of Justice, </span></b><a href="https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/jurisprudence?publishedId=C-188%2F24&amp;searchTerm=C%252D188%252F24"><b><i><span data-contrast="none">Coyote System</span></i></b></a><b><i><span data-contrast="auto"> </span></i></b><b><span data-contrast="auto">(Joined Cases C-188/24 and C-190/24), </span></b><b><span data-contrast="auto">is concerning: it could deprive online platforms of liability protection because of how they organize and disseminate user content. The consequences for freedom of expression could be significant.</span></b><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<h3><span data-ccp-props="true}"><span data-contrast="none" lang="EN-GB" class="TextRun MacChromeBold SCXW231073070 BCX0" xml:lang="EN-GB"><span class="NormalTextRun SCXW231073070 BCX0" data-ccp-parastyle="pdq2pg_selectionanchorcontainer" data-ccp-parastyle-defn="[201342446,&quot;1&quot;,201342447,&quot;5&quot;,201342448,&quot;1&quot;,201342449,&quot;1&quot;,469777841,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777842,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777843,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777844,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,201341986,&quot;1&quot;,469769226,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,268442635,&quot;24&quot;,469775450,&quot;pdq2pg_selectionanchorcontainer&quot;,201340122,&quot;2&quot;,134233614,&quot;true&quot;,469778129,&quot;pdq2pgselectionanchorcontainer&quot;,335572020,&quot;1&quot;,335559705,&quot;2057&quot;,134233118,&quot;true&quot;,134233117,&quot;true&quot;,469778324,&quot;Normal&quot;]}">Liability Protections in the EU</span></span><span class="EOP Selected SCXW231073070 BCX0" data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></span></h3>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">The European Union has long embraced a system of limited liability for online service providers. Under the e-Commerce Directive and now the </span><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Services_Act"><span data-contrast="none">Digital Services Act (DSA)</span></a><span data-contrast="auto">, platforms benefit from liability exemptions for user content. To discourage censorship, they also cannot be required to generally monitor user content or actively search for illegal activity. But that liability protection comes with </span>qualifications<span data-contrast="auto">: Platforms </span><span data-contrast="auto">lose this benefit if they play an "active role" such that they have </span>knowledge of, or control over, user-provided information<span data-contrast="auto"> (Recital 42 ECD, Recital 18 DSA, and case law, for example para. 113 in </span><a href="https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62024CC0188"><i><span data-contrast="none">L’Oréal v eBay)</span></i></a><span data-contrast="auto">. </span><span data-contrast="auto">For hosting services, providers must remove or disable content they know to be illegal. The DSA has introduced extensive due diligence obligations for platforms but left these foundational immunities intact. </span><b><span data-contrast="auto">The message is clear: platforms bear responsibility for proper systems and processes, but generally not for users' speech.</span></b><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<p><i><span data-contrast="auto">Coyote System</span></i><span data-contrast="auto">, however, could </span>undermine this balance.<b><span data-contrast="auto"> </span></b><span data-contrast="auto">Confronted with a case about restrictions on navigation systems that transmit information to drivers about roadside checks, the Court formulated a general test for when an intermediary ceases to be a "neutral" host and therefore loses the hosting liability exemption. In essence, the Court held that where an intermediary's algorithm goes beyond merely categorizing and indexing user information to determine, "</span><i><span data-contrast="auto">under what conditions, how and in which order of priority</span></i><span data-contrast="auto">" (para. 122) information is disseminated, the intermediary "controls" that information and is deprived of protection under the e-Commerce Directive.</span><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">Let's be clear: the case is </span><span data-contrast="auto">not about a service that ranked or recommended user-generated content in the way social media platforms do.</span><span data-contrast="auto"> It is about the collection and real-time relay of user alerts about roadside checks.</span><b><span data-contrast="auto"> </span></b><span data-contrast="auto">However, </span><b><span data-contrast="auto">the Court's reasoning is not confined to navigation services. </span></b><span data-contrast="auto">Recommendation algorithms determine how and in what order user content is disseminated across virtually every major online platform. </span><span data-contrast="auto">Should such platforms now cease to qualify as neutral intermediaries and lose the protection of the hosting liability exemption? The answer should be no.</span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<h3><span data-ccp-props="259}"><span data-contrast="auto" lang="EN-GB" class="TextRun MacChromeBold SCXW169279319 BCX0" xml:lang="EN-GB"><span class="NormalTextRun SCXW169279319 BCX0" data-ccp-parastyle="isselectedend" data-ccp-parastyle-defn="[201342446,&quot;1&quot;,201342447,&quot;5&quot;,201342448,&quot;1&quot;,201342449,&quot;1&quot;,469777841,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777842,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777843,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777844,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,201341986,&quot;1&quot;,469769226,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,268442635,&quot;24&quot;,469775450,&quot;isselectedend&quot;,201340122,&quot;2&quot;,134233614,&quot;true&quot;,469778129,&quot;isselectedend&quot;,335572020,&quot;1&quot;,335559705,&quot;2057&quot;,134233118,&quot;true&quot;,134233117,&quot;true&quot;,469778324,&quot;Normal&quot;]}">The Meaning of Control</span></span><span class="EOP Selected SCXW169279319 BCX0" data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></span></h3>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">Control has never been understood this broadly. Nor should it be. Every hosting service provider, think of Facebook, Amazon or Bluesky, will have some control over users’ content. If that ability alone ruled the analysis, the liability exemption would become largely meaningless. Instead, the disqualifying “active role” must relate to the actual content itself, not merely the technical means by which that content is organised or disseminated.</span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">The Court’s own case law reinforces this conclusion: </span><span data-contrast="auto">In </span><a href="https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:62018CJ0682"><i><span data-contrast="none">YouTube and Cyando</span></i></a><span data-contrast="auto">, it examined a platform that categorises, ranks and recommends user content through algorithms, yet still proceeded on the basis that it could generally benefit from the hosting liability exemption. To be sure, the Court was mainly addressing specific knowledge of illegal content rather than the separate category of control. Even so, the underlying premise is clear: </span><b><span data-contrast="auto">those features do not, by themselves, place a platform outside of protection</span></b><span data-contrast="auto">. Advocate General therefore </span><a href="https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/document?source=document&amp;text=&amp;docid=228712&amp;pageIndex=0&amp;doclang=en&amp;mode=req&amp;dir=&amp;occ=first&amp;part=1"><span data-contrast="none">explained</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> that what matters is the provider's "intellectual control of that content" (para 152). The relevant question is who controls the information itself, makes it their own, not who determines how it appears.</span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<p><b><span data-contrast="auto">That is precisely where </span></b><b><i><span data-contrast="auto">Coyote System</span></i></b><b><span data-contrast="auto"> breaks new ground and offers a dangerous </span></b><a href="https://eulawanalysis.blogspot.com/2026/06/the-end-of-immunity-for-internet.html?m=1"><b><span data-contrast="none">change of emphasis</span></b></a><b><span data-contrast="auto">. By equating algorithmic organisation with content control, the ruling risks excluding social networks and other platforms from the liability exemption and encouraging proactive monitoring of what users say online and removal of lawful content.</span></b><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">That outcome would have terrible consequences for freedom of expression in the EU. It’s also </span><b><span data-contrast="auto">difficult to reconcile with the structure of the DSA</span></b><span data-contrast="auto">, which certainly does not treat recommendation algorithms as incompatible with intermediary immunity. On the contrary, it accepts them as a defining feature of modern platforms, regulates them extensively through dedicated due diligence obligations, and still leaves the hosting liability regime untouched (it even integrated the </span><i><span data-contrast="auto">YouTube </span></i><span data-contrast="auto">ruling in its preamble!). </span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">This was no accident: During the DSA negotiations, proposals to deprive platforms of the hosting liability exemption if they optimize, classify, organize or otherwise promote online content were rejected, following successful </span><a href="https://www.eff.org/document/dsa-joint-letter-ep"><span data-contrast="none">advocacy by EFF and allies</span></a><span data-contrast="auto">. Would the Court have decided this case differently under the DSA? Probably not. It’s more plausible that the EU judges were influenced by the specific nature of the service, which could explain why the judgment says remarkably, and sadly, little about why intermediary liability exists in the first place and the fundamental rights it serves. </span><i><span data-contrast="auto">Coyote System</span></i><span data-contrast="auto"> did not merely transmit user reports but aggregated them into what the Advocate General </span><a href="https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/jurisprudence?publishedId=C-188%2F24&amp;searchTerm=C-188%2F24"><span data-contrast="none">described</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> as a new "information layer," a distinction </span><a href="https://verfassungsblog.de/eugh-coyote-russmedia-haftung-plattformen/"><span data-contrast="none">omitted</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> by the Court.</span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<h3><span data-ccp-props="259}"><span class="EOP Selected SCXW169279319 BCX0" data-ccp-props="true}"><span data-contrast="auto" lang="EN-GB" class="TextRun MacChromeBold SCXW233784235 BCX0" xml:lang="EN-GB"><span class="NormalTextRun SCXW233784235 BCX0" data-ccp-parastyle="isselectedend" data-ccp-parastyle-defn="[201342446,&quot;1&quot;,201342447,&quot;5&quot;,201342448,&quot;1&quot;,201342449,&quot;1&quot;,469777841,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777842,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777843,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,469777844,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,201341986,&quot;1&quot;,469769226,&quot;Times New Roman&quot;,268442635,&quot;24&quot;,469775450,&quot;isselectedend&quot;,201340122,&quot;2&quot;,134233614,&quot;true&quot;,469778129,&quot;isselectedend&quot;,335572020,&quot;1&quot;,335559705,&quot;2057&quot;,134233118,&quot;true&quot;,134233117,&quot;true&quot;,469778324,&quot;Normal&quot;]}">Chipping Away </span><span class="NormalTextRun SCXW233784235 BCX0" data-ccp-parastyle="isselectedend">at </span><span class="NormalTextRun SCXW233784235 BCX0" data-ccp-parastyle="isselectedend">Intermediary Liability Protections</span></span><span class="EOP Selected SCXW233784235 BCX0" data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></span></span></h3>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">The danger is that the Court's broad language on algorithmic curation reaches well beyond that narrow category and, </span><span data-contrast="auto">unintentionally or not, </span><b><span data-contrast="auto">chips away at one of the most important safeguards for freedom of expression online.</span></b><span data-contrast="auto"> </span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">Unfortunately, </span><i><span data-contrast="auto">Coyote System</span></i><span data-contrast="auto"> does not stand alone. It is the latest in a </span><b><span data-contrast="auto">line of judgments that have gradually narrowed intermediary liability protections</span></b><span data-contrast="auto">. Recently, in </span><a href="https://verfassungsblog.de/cjeu-russmedia/"><i><span data-contrast="none">Russmedia</span></i></a><span data-contrast="auto">, the Court privileged preventive content control in the name of data protection, paying </span><a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6966821"><span data-contrast="none">little regard</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> to the possibility of reconciling both regimes and the privacy costs of increased monitoring of user content. And in </span><a href="https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/affair?lang=en&amp;sort=AFF_NUM-DESC&amp;searchTerm=%2522C%252D421%252F24%2522&amp;publishedId=C-421%2F24"><i><span data-contrast="none">AGCOM</span></i></a><span data-contrast="auto">, concerning Google's liability for YouTube videos uploaded by creators participating in its Partner Programme, the Court appears to leap from eligibility reviews to specific knowledge of illegal content.</span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">There is a</span><b><span data-contrast="auto"> political risk too.</span></b><span data-contrast="auto"> While the top court’s reasoning will be applied by national courts and further refined over time, the European Commission has shown little hesitation in incorporating landmark rulings into legislation. Just recently, in its digital omnibus proposal, it selectively restated part of a recent Court of Justice </span><a href="https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex:62023CJ0413"><span data-contrast="none">judgment</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> to </span><a href="https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/12/eus-new-digital-package-proposal-promises-red-tape-cuts-guts-gdpr-privacy-rights"><span data-contrast="none">justify narrowing privacy rights</span></a><span data-contrast="auto"> of users.</span><span data-ccp-props="true}"> </span></p>

<p><span data-contrast="auto">If these trends continue, freedom of expression online will become collateral damage in the EU.</span><span data-ccp-props="259}"> </span></p>
